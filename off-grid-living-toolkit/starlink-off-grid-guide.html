Starlink Mini Off-Grid Guide

Internet at a camp with no wired connection — what the Mini needs, what it uses, and how to keep it running on generator power.

At an off-grid camp, internet is not a luxury any more. It is how you check the weather, order parts, talk to people, and run a business from a place with no wired connection. The Starlink Mini is made for that kind of use: a small dish with the router built in, meant to be carried, set up, and run on modest power. This guide covers the Mini on the Roam plan, at a camp with generator-only electricity.

What the Mini is, and what Roam means

The Starlink Mini is a compact satellite dish with a built-in Wi-Fi router. You point it at a clear patch of sky, plug it in, and it connects to Starlink satellites overhead. On the Roam plan, you can use it in different places, not just at one fixed home address, and in-motion use is included on Roam. That matters at a camp: you can move the dish around the property to find a clearer view. Check your own plan details for how much data is included.

Power: the numbers that matter

The Mini runs on 12 to 48 volts DC. It uses about 25 to 40 watts on average, and up to 60 watts at most. That is low compared with a full-size home dish, and it is the reason the Mini works well off-grid. At 40 watts, running it for 10 hours uses about 400 watt-hours — roughly the same as a small fridge for part of a day, and far less than an electric heater. But it is still a steady load that does not turn itself off, so leaving it on all day and night adds up. Use the solar and battery estimator and the generator runtime calculator on the main toolkit page to work out what that costs you in fuel and battery capacity, and decide whether to switch the dish off overnight.

What is in the kit — and what is not

The Mini kit comes with an AC power supply and a 15 metre DC cable. That is what you get in the box. It does not include a 12V car adapter, and it does not include a USB-C cable for running the dish from a battery or vehicle. Those are separate accessories. If you plan to run the Mini straight from a 12V battery bank or a vehicle, you will need one of those accessories — the AC supply on its own needs mains power, which usually means running the generator or an inverter. Check what you actually have before you assume you can plug the dish straight into a battery. A short test at home, before you rely on it at camp, saves a cold night with no internet.

Mounting and a clear view of the sky

The dish needs a clear view of the sky. Trees, a roof, a hill, or a metal building in the way will cut the signal or drop it altogether. At camp, that usually means mounting it up high or out in the open: on a roof rack, a pole, a post, or a flat surface with nothing above it. A mount also keeps it out of the snow and off the ground. Fasten it properly — a dish that blows over stops working. After each move, give it a few minutes to find the satellites again.

Snow, cold, and weather

Snow on the dish blocks the signal, just like a tree does. The dish has a snow-melt setting that warms the face to clear light snow, but that setting uses extra power — sometimes a lot of extra power — which matters when every watt comes from a generator or a battery. In heavy snow, clearing it by hand is often cheaper than melting it electrically. Keep the cable from being buried in snow or pinched under a door, and keep the connections dry.

Budgeting power on generator-only electricity

When the generator is your only power, every load is a choice. The Mini at 25 to 40 watts is a small load, but it runs for hours. Work it into your daily plan the same way you plan fuel for lights and charging: decide when the internet needs to be on, and switch it off when it does not. Running the generator for the dish alone is wasteful, so charge batteries, run the pump, and use the internet in the same generator window when you can. If you have solar as well, the dish is a good daytime load, because it uses power while the sun is up and the panels are producing. Overnight, switching it off saves battery for the things that cannot wait until morning.
