The Off-Grid Living Toolkit was built by Lucas Norman, who is wintering at his camp at McArthur Lake near Timmins, Ontario. It comes from real off-grid living, not guesswork, and not from reading about it somewhere else. The calculators and guides on this site are the ones he needed for his own camp, and the numbers in them are the numbers he has had to work out in real life — how much firewood a winter takes, how long a tote of water lasts, how much fuel a generator burns, and how much propane a heater uses.

Why this site exists

Living off-grid in northern Ontario in winter is a practical problem before it is anything else. The power comes from a generator. The heat comes from firewood. The water comes from totes outside, and the hot water comes from a propane tankless heater. The internet comes from a small satellite dish. None of that is automatic, and none of it forgives guessing. A camp that runs out of wood in February, or runs out of water in a snowstorm, or has a generator that will not start on a cold morning, is in real trouble.

This site exists to make that planning easier. The calculators give you a number you can work with — how many cords, how many days, how many gallons, how many watts — instead of a guess. The guides explain the thinking behind the numbers, in plain language, so you can see why a seasoned cord of jack pine gives more heat than a cord of poplar, why a pitcher pump will not run a tankless water heater, and why a battery that works fine in summer can let you down in winter.

What is here

The main page has five calculators: a firewood calculator, a cabin solar and battery estimator, a generator runtime calculator, a propane usage calculator, and a water supply calculator. There is also a short winterizing checklist that covers the basics — water, heat, power, propane, and access — because winter makes small problems big, and the checklist is the fastest way to catch a problem before it becomes one.

The guides go deeper. The firewood guide covers seasoned softwood, drying and stacking, chimney cleaning, and backup heat. The winter water guide covers water totes, freezing, draining lines, and hot water. The generator and solar guide covers generator-only power, fuel and servicing, batteries in the cold, solar, and Starlink Mini power. Each guide links back to the calculator it belongs to, so you can read the guide and then work out your own numbers.

What this site is not

This site is not a replacement for a professional. Gas work in Ontario must be done by a TSSA-licensed gas fitter, and electrical work, chimney work, and structural work all have their own rules and their own risks. The calculators and guides here are planning tools, and the figures in them are estimates. Your stove, your weather, your insulation, and your actual equipment will change the real numbers. If you are not sure about a gas, electrical, or structural job, get a licensed professional to look at it.

This site is also not a shop. There are no products for sale here. The guides do have Amazon affiliate links for gear actually used off-grid, clearly labelled as affiliate links, as explained in the privacy page. Advertising may appear on the site through Google AdSense, and that advertising is what helps keep the toolkit free to use.

The camp behind the site

The camp at McArthur Lake is a real place, lived in through a real northern winter. The wood is softwood — spruce, balsam fir, jack pine, and poplar — cut, split, and stacked by hand. The power is generator-only, with batteries and solar to help. The water is carried in totes, and the hot water is heated by propane. The winterizing checklist on the main page is not a theory; it is the list of things that have to be right before the cold arrives, learned the hard way when something was not right.

That is the point of the site. If you are setting up a camp, winterizing a cabin, or trying to work out whether you have enough wood, water, fuel, or power to get through a season, the tools here are meant to help you answer that question with a number, not a hope. Use them, check them against your own experience, and plan for the worst month, not the best one.

How the site is kept going

The toolkit is free to use, and it is kept going by advertising. The site is built on free hosting, and the calculators run entirely in your browser — nothing you type into them is sent to a server. That is a deliberate choice, so the site stays fast, simple, and private to use, even on a slow camp internet connection.
