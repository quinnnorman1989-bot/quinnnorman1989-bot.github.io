How to Declutter Before a Move

Every item you get rid of now is a box you don't pack, carry, pay to move, and unpack. Declutter first, pack second.

Moving is the best excuse you'll ever have to get rid of things. You're handling every item you own anyway, so the question is simple: is this worth packing, carrying, and finding a place for in the new home? If the answer isn't a clear yes, it goes. The trick is having a system, so you're deciding quickly instead of standing in a room for an hour holding a mug you forgot you owned.

The four piles: Keep, Donate, Sell, Discard

Every item goes into one of four piles. Keep means it earns its place — you use it, you need it, or it genuinely matters to you. Donate means it's in good shape and someone else could use it: clothes, books, kitchenware, toys, furniture. Sell means it's worth enough to be worth the effort of listing it, and you have time to sell it before the move. Discard means it's broken, worn out, expired, or not worth giving away. Be honest with yourself about the sell pile: if you haven't listed it within a week, move it to donate. A sell pile that never sells is just clutter with a plan that didn't happen. PackRight tracks all four categories in the app — when you add an item, you can sort it as Keep, Donate, Sell, or Discard right there.

Go room by room, and finish each one

Start with the easiest room, not the hardest. The bathroom or a storage closet is a good first win: expired medicines, old toiletries, and things you forgot existed. Finishing one room gives you momentum for the next. Don't bounce between rooms — half-decluttered rooms everywhere is how you end up packing things you meant to get rid of. Work through the house in order: storage spaces first, then bedrooms, then the living areas, and the kitchen last, since you use it every day. Set a timer if it helps — an hour per room, with a break between rooms. Decluttering is tiring, and tired people keep everything.

The questions that make decisions easy

For each item, ask: have I used this in the last year? Would I buy it again today? Does it work, and do I have another one that does the same job? If I moved without it, would I notice? For clothes, the hanger test works well: turn all your hangers one way, and when you wear something, turn its hanger back. After a few weeks, anything still facing the wrong way is a candidate for donate. For sentimental items, keep the best one, not all of them — one box of childhood keepsakes, not five. Take a photo of things you want to remember but don't need to keep. The photo takes up no space in the truck.

Get it out of the house the same week

This is the step that makes or breaks the whole job. A donate pile that sits in the hallway for three weeks will get picked through, and half of it will end up back on a shelf. Bag it, box it, and get it out the same week: drop donations off, put discards in the bin or take them to the dump, and list sell items right away. If you're selling furniture, photograph it and list it early — big items take the longest to sell, and you don't want to be giving them away on moving day because the buyer never showed up. For hazardous things like old paint, batteries, and chemicals, check your local waste depot's rules instead of putting them in the regular bin.

What to do with paper, photos, and the junk drawer

Paper is the heaviest clutter nobody notices. Shred old bills and statements you no longer need, recycle old magazines and catalogues, and scan or photograph anything you need to keep. The junk drawer gets the same treatment as everything else: empty it completely, throw out the dried pens and dead batteries, and only put back what you actually use. Photos deserve their own afternoon — sort them, keep the good ones, digitise the ones that matter, and let the blurry duplicates go.

Declutter first, pack second

Decluttering isn't a separate job from packing — it's the first step of packing. Do it before you buy boxes, before you wrap a single plate, and the whole move gets smaller, cheaper, and faster.
