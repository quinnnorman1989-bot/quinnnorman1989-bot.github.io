First Night Essentials Box — What to Pack

One box, packed last and opened first, with everything you need to sleep, eat, wash, and function on your first night in a new home.

You arrive at the new place tired, the truck is half-unloaded, and every box looks the same. The First Night Essentials box is the one box that fixes that. Pack it last, label it clearly, keep it with you rather than buried in the truck, and open it before anything else. It holds everything you need for the first night and the first morning — nothing more, nothing less.

Bedding and sleep

This is the part people forget and regret most. Pack bedding for every bed: sheets, pillows, blankets or a duvet, and a change of clothes for the morning. If you don't pack bedding in the essentials box, you'll be digging through boxes at midnight looking for a pillow, or sleeping on a bare mattress. Pack the bedding on top of the box so it's the first thing out, and make the beds as soon as the frames are in place. A good night's sleep makes the whole first week easier; a bad one makes everything harder.

Toiletries and medications

Pack what you'd take on an overnight trip: toothbrush and toothpaste, soap, shampoo, deodorant, any medication you take daily, contact lens supplies or glasses, and a towel per person. Don't forget toilet paper — the new place won't have any, and it's the single most commonly forgotten item on moving day. Add paper towels, hand soap for the bathroom and kitchen sinks, and a small first-aid kit with plasters and painkillers. Keep any prescription medication in your personal bag, not in a box in the truck, so it's always within reach.

Food, drink, and the kettle

You won't want to cook a real meal on moving night, and you won't find the kitchen boxes anyway. Pack the kettle or coffee maker, a few mugs, a couple of plates and bowls, cutlery, a can opener, and some basic food: coffee or tea, snacks, bottled water, and something simple for dinner, whether that's a takeout menu for a place near the new house or a few tins and some pasta. Add dish soap, a sponge, and a tea towel so you can wash up. A small cool bag with milk, butter, and anything that needs to stay cold is worth bringing if the fridge is running at the new place.

Power, tools, and documents

Your phone is your lifeline on moving day — for directions, for calling the movers, for the keys. Pack your phone charger and a charged power bank in the essentials box, not in a random box in the truck. Add a flashlight, a screwdriver, a box cutter or scissors for opening boxes, bin bags for rubbish, and some basic cleaning supplies. Keep your important documents with you: the new lease or keys paperwork, your ID, and any moving paperwork. A small amount of cash is useful too, for tipping movers or buying a pizza when the kitchen is still in boxes.

For kids and pets

If you have kids, pack their essentials separately in the same box or in a bag they can carry: a favourite toy or blanket, pyjamas, a change of clothes, snacks, and anything they need to settle at bedtime. For pets, pack food and water bowls, a few days of food, a lead, litter and a tray for cats, and a blanket that smells like home. Moving is stressful for animals too, and having their things ready makes the first night calmer for everyone.

How to pack the box itself

Use a sturdy box that's easy to carry, and don't overfill it. Put the bedding in last so it comes out first, or pack bedding in a separate bag on top. Label the box "FIRST NIGHT — OPEN FIRST" in big letters on two sides, and tell anyone helping you move that this box stays with you. It goes in your car, not the truck, and it comes into the bedroom first when you arrive.
