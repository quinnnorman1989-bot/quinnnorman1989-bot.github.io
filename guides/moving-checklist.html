Moving Checklist: 8 Weeks to Moving Day

A week-by-week plan for a move that doesn't fall apart in the last 48 hours. Print it, or track it in the PackRight app.

Most moves go wrong in the same way: nothing happens for six weeks, then everything happens at once. The fix is boring and it works — start early, do a little every week, and keep one list that everything goes on. This checklist is built around eight weeks, but if you only have four, just compress the first two stages into the first week and keep the order the same.

8 weeks out: decide and declutter

This is the week for decisions, not boxes. Confirm your moving date and how you're moving — hired movers, a rented truck, or friends with a pickup. If you're hiring movers, get the booking in now; good crews fill up, especially at the end of the month. Start decluttering room by room before you pack a single box. Everything you get rid of now is a box you don't pack, carry, and unpack. Sort as you go into keep, donate, sell, and discard, and get the donate and discard piles out of the house the same week, or they will creep back onto the shelves. Our decluttering guide walks through this step in detail.

Also start a moving folder — paper or digital — for quotes, receipts, and your new address details. If you're renting, check your lease for the notice you owe and the condition the place has to be left in.

6 weeks out: gather supplies and start packing what you don't use

Get your packing supplies in one trip: sturdy boxes in a few sizes, packing tape, markers, packing paper or bubble wrap, and stretch wrap for furniture. Small boxes for heavy things like books, big boxes for light things like bedding — a big box full of books is a box nobody can lift. Start packing the things you won't miss: out-of-season clothes, books, decor, spare kitchen gear, and the contents of storage closets. Number each box and write its destination room on at least two sides, not just the top, because boxes get stacked and the top disappears. PackRight's box labelling guide explains a system that makes unpacking fast instead of a treasure hunt.

4 weeks out: change your address and book the services

Change your address with the post office, your bank, your employer, your insurance, and any subscriptions. Book the practical things people forget: internet and utilities at the new place, with the start date lined up to moving day so you're not moving into a dark house with no water. If you have kids or pets, sort out who looks after them on moving day — a house full of open doors and heavy boxes is no place for either. Measure the big furniture against the doorways and stairwells at the new place. Finding out the couch doesn't fit on moving day is a bad time to learn it.

2 weeks out: pack room by room

Now pack in earnest, one room at a time, and finish each room before starting the next. Half-packed rooms everywhere is how things get lost. Pack a box, seal it, label it, and move it to a staging area near the door so moving day is carrying, not packing. Keep a running inventory of what's in each numbered box — this is exactly what PackRight is for: every item, its box, and its room, searchable later when you're standing in the new kitchen asking where the coffee maker is. Set aside anything you'll need in the last few days and the first night, and don't pack it. That list becomes your First Night Essentials box.

1 week out: confirm everything

Confirm the movers or the truck, the start time, and the address — both addresses. Confirm the keys for the new place and who hands them over. Pack the kitchen last among the big rooms, since you use it every day, and read our kitchen packing guide before you start, because dishes and glasses are where most breakage happens. Defrost the freezer a day or two before the move if you're taking it. Do a load of laundry so you're not moving a hamper of dirty clothes. Charge your phone and a power bank.

Moving day

Walk the old place with the movers and point out anything fragile or awkward. Keep your essentials box, your documents, your phone charger, and your wallet with you — not in the truck. Do a final sweep of every room, every closet, every cupboard, and the garage before you lock up. At the new place, put the essentials box in the bedroom first, get the beds made, and get the kettle working. Everything else can wait until tomorrow.

The first week in

Unpack the kitchen and bedrooms first; the living room and decor can wait. Break down boxes as you empty them so cardboard doesn't take over the house. Update your address anywhere you missed, and keep your moving receipts together — some moving costs are tax-deductible in specific situations, and your receipts are the proof either way.
